In 1924, an unknown professor in Dhaka mailed Albert Einstein a paper — and quietly rewrote physics. This is Satyendra Nath Bose (1894–1974), in 30 seconds.

A Bear's Notes mini-bio.

The story: Bose rederived Planck's law of glowing heat using pure quantum statistics, with no classical assumptions. Einstein saw it instantly, translated it, and got it published. The rule counts how many identical particles can crowd into each energy state — and particles that obey it, the bosons, can pile into a single quantum state. With Einstein, Bose predicted a whole new form of matter, the Bose–Einstein condensate, finally created in a lab in 1995. An entire class of particles carries his name — yet Bose never won a Nobel Prize.

Key idea — Bose–Einstein distribution:  n̄ᵢ = 1 / (e^((Eᵢ−μ)/k_BT) − 1)
