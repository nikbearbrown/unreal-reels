TITLE: Photosynthesis

A leaf is a quiet machine for catching light. Inside its cells sit chloroplasts, packed with a
green pigment called chlorophyll that absorbs sunlight — mostly the red and blue, which is why a
leaf looks green. That captured energy splits water into hydrogen and oxygen; the oxygen drifts
out into the air we breathe. The hydrogen is kept, and used to staple carbon dioxide pulled from
the air into sugar. So the whole trick is this: light plus water plus carbon dioxide, rearranged
into sugar and oxygen. Every calorie you have ever eaten started as sunlight a leaf decided to
keep.
