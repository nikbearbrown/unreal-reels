Why a Particle in a Box Cannot Sit Still — Bear's Doodles


Drop a marble into a box and it settles on the floor, perfectly still. Try the same with a quantum particle, and it refuses to ever hold still.

Trap the particle between two hard walls; its wave must fall to zero at each. A perfectly flat, still wave would have to be zero everywhere — so nothing is there. The smallest wave that fits is one gentle arch, peaking in the middle, zero at the walls. That arch is curved, and a quantum wave's curvature is exactly its kinetic energy. A flatter wave would carry less energy, but the walls forbid going completely flat. So the lowest allowed state still bulges and curves — it still carries motion. Squeeze the box narrower and the arch sharpens, forcing the energy even higher. It can never sit still; confinement itself guarantees a restless lowest state.
