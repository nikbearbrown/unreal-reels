## Description
Physicists call an energy eigenstate 'stationary' because nothing you can measure about it ever changes. But peek underneath, and the wave function is spinning the entire time.

The wave function lives in the complex plane: a real axis and an imaginary one. An energy eigenstate is an arrow in that plane — a clock hand. It spins steadily, at a rate set entirely by its energy. Its real and imaginary parts oscillate ninety degrees apart, like the shadow of a turning hand. But the hand's length never changes — the tip just traces a circle. Probability is that length squared — and squaring throws the spinning angle away. So the hand whirls on the left while the only thing you can observe sits frozen on the right. That's the trick of the word: stationary on the outside, spinning underneath.
