Why a Dim Blue Lamp Beats a Blinding Red One — Bear's Doodles

Blast a metal plate with the brightest red lamp you can find — not one electron escapes. Switch to a faint blue glow, and electrons fly off the instant it touches.

Light doesn't arrive as a smooth stream; it comes in separate packets. Each packet's energy is fixed by its colour — red packets weak, blue packets strong. Freeing an electron takes one packet landing enough energy in a single hit. A red packet strikes, falls short, and bounces off — the electron stays put. Pile on millions of red packets and still nothing; weak hits never add up. One faint blue packet lands enough in a single blow — an electron flies free. Brightness only adds more packets; a packet's colour decides if it can free anything. So light's energy comes in chunks, and one chunk's colour frees the electron.
