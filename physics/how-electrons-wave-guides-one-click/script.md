How an Electron's Wave Guides One Click

A00. An electron does not carry a tiny route card through space.
A01. Before we look, its wave spreads across possible landing places.
A02. The wave is not extra electron material poured everywhere.
A03. It is a probability landscape for one future click.
A04. High parts of the landscape mean more likely here.
A05. Low parts mean possible, but much less likely.
A06. When a detector checks, one red dot appears.
A07. The whole landscape collapses into that single result.
A08. Run the same setup again, and another dot appears.
A09. After many runs, the dots trace the wave's shape.
A10. So the wave guides the statistics, not a hidden bead.
A11. The hard work is predicting how that wave moves.
A12. Quantum mechanics is the rulebook for that moving landscape.
A13. One spread-out possibility map can steer one sharp detection.
