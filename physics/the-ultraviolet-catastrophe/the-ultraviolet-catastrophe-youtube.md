Two Cowork skills that turn a textbook into doodle-style explainer videos.

Scout finds the ideas — scans a book for high-value concepts where motion actually teaches something, ranks them, and drops candidate cards into video-ideas.md. You pick one and hand it to the builder.

Builder runs a phase-gated pipeline: script → beat sheet → audio → Manim render → doodle assembly → publish package. Nine steps, most scripted; the only manual work is drawing the doodle clips and doing final assembly in Adobe Rush/Premiere.

The core architectural split: Manim handles everything exact (graphs, equations, text, placeholder markers for where doodles go). Doodles are hand-drawn characters animated stroke-by-stroke in a doodle napkin art style — they live outside Manim because Manim can't ingest mp4. Rush/Premiere is where the two tracks merge.

Audio-first discipline throughout: real ElevenLabs clip durations drive animation timing, not estimates. One sentence per beat.


This video "The Ultraviolet Catastrophe"

Everything warm is quietly glowing — your body, a campfire, the sun — all giving off light. Our best classical theory predicted that glow should blast you with endless ultraviolet — but it doesn't.

Plot how brightly an object glows against the frequency of the light it emits. At low frequencies, only a few light-wave patterns fit inside the object. But the number of possible patterns climbs faster and faster as the frequency rises. Classical physics hands every single pattern the exact same share of energy. Countless patterns, each fully funded, drive the predicted brightness screaming toward infinity. But real objects stay calm — their glow rises, peaks, and gently falls. The fix is a strange rule: energy comes only in whole chunks, never slivers. Each chunk costs more at higher frequency — ultraviolet light is wildly expensive. The warm object simply cannot afford those costly high-frequency patterns. So the expensive patterns stay empty, and the runaway curve bends back to zero. That one rule — energy in chunks — lit the fuse of all quantum physics.
