# The Ultraviolet Catastrophe — Narration Script (Gate 1)

*Source: Candidate 02, `quantum-mechanics-vol1/chapters/01-why-classical-physics-failed.md`. Arc: Hook → Accumulation → Reveal → Implication. One sentence = one beat. Two scenes (CUT before beat 2, CUT before beat 7). ~12 beats + intro ≈ 80–90s.*

**Production: mostly Manim, two doodles.** The rule for which register:

- **Manim** — vector objects with no personality: axes, walls, curves, waves, energy chunks, the price tag, the runaway, the bending curve. The whole graph-and-simulation spine.
- **Doodle** — a character *with personality* (a person, creature, the Bear's Doodles mascot), drawn stroke-by-stroke from an Ale-Giorgini-style image. Never forced — the intro gets one, and body beats only when a character genuinely earns the moment.

The personality moments are the intro and the hook. The hook is **two doodle beats** that motivate the problem before any theory: everything warm glows (why you should care), and our best theory predicted that glow should fry you (the stakes). Only then does the Manim plot explain why it doesn't.

| Beat | Register | Narration | Drawn element |
|---|---|---|---|
| **INTRO** | Doodle | "Bear's Doodles" [pause] "The Ultraviolet Catastrophe" | a Bear's Doodles character draws in + title |
| H01 | **Doodle** | Everything warm is quietly glowing — your body, a campfire, the sun — all giving off light. | a person warming by a campfire |
| H02 | **Doodle** | Our best classical theory predicted that glow should blast you with endless ultraviolet — but it doesn't. | a person bracing as ultraviolet rays blast at them |
| 2 | Manim | [CUT] Plot how brightly an object glows against the frequency of the light it emits. | axes: frequency →, brightness ↑ |
| 3 | Manim | At low frequencies, only a few light-wave patterns fit inside the object. | 2–3 standing-wave modes at the low-frequency end |
| 4 | Manim | But the number of possible patterns climbs faster and faster as the frequency rises. | mode count rising steeply (∝ f²) |
| 5 | Manim | Classical physics hands every single pattern the exact same share of energy. | equal-height energy bars on each mode |
| 6 | Manim | Countless patterns, each fully funded, drive the predicted brightness screaming toward infinity. | Rayleigh–Jeans curve shooting up off-frame |
| 7 | Manim | [CUT] But real objects stay calm — their glow rises, peaks, and gently falls. | the measured curve: rise, peak, fall |
| 8 | Manim | The fix is a strange rule: energy comes only in whole chunks, never slivers. | energy as discrete stacked chunks vs. a smooth ramp |
| 9 | Manim | Each chunk costs more at higher frequency — ultraviolet light is wildly expensive. | chunk size growing with frequency; a price tag on the tallest |
| 10 | Manim | The warm object simply cannot afford those costly high-frequency patterns. | high-frequency modes greying out / going empty |
| 11 | Manim | So the expensive patterns stay empty, and the runaway curve bends back to zero. | Planck curve bending down, overlaid on the faint runaway |
| 12 | Manim | That one rule — energy in chunks — lit the fuse of all quantum physics. | the finished Planck curve, one chunk highlighted |
