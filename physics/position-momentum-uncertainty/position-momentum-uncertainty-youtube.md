Why You Can't Pin Down Position and Momentum at Once — Bear's Doodles

You've heard you can't know a particle's exact position and speed at the same time. The usual story blames the measuring device for disturbing it — but that's not the real reason.

Show the particle two ways: where it is, and which wavelengths it's made of. A wide, spread-out wave has one clear wavelength, so its momentum is sharp. But spread out like that, you can barely say where the particle is. Now squeeze the wave to pin its position down to a tight spike. Building that spike takes a whole mix of different wavelengths added together. So the momentum panel fans out wide exactly as the position panel narrows. Pin either one tighter and the other must spread; their product never shrinks past a limit. No microscope touched it — the trade-off is the wave's own shape, before any measurement.
