## Description
A laser emits only certain exact colors, and an atom holds only certain exact energies. It turns out those two facts are really one and the same.

Trap a wave between two walls. Pick a wave at random and it won't reach zero at the walls. On each round trip it lands out of step with itself and cancels to nothing. Only a wave that vanishes at both walls survives. Now every round trip lands in step, reinforcing into a stable standing wave. Only a discrete set of waves fits — one hump, two, three, and so on. In an atom those surviving modes are the allowed energies; in a laser cavity they're the allowed colors. Same fact, twice: only the waves that fit are allowed to exist.
