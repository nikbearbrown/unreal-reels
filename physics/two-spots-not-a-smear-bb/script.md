## Act 1 — The plate (HOOK)

1. In 1922, Otto Stern and Walther Gerlach fired silver atoms through a lopsided magnetic field.
2. Each atom is a tiny magnet, and every classical magnet can point any direction it likes.
3. So the field should smear the beam into one continuous vertical streak.
4. The plate showed two sharp spots — and nothing at all in between.
5. The atoms had no right to land in only two places.

## Act 2 — Why two (INSTANCE)

6. The field pushes each atom according to the magnet's tilt along the field axis.
7. Continuous tilts would mean continuous pushes — that is the classical streak.
8. Two spots mean the tilt itself comes in exactly two values, up or down.
9. Nature was announcing that spin along any axis is a two-answer question.
10. The Born rule is not abstract here — it is written in silver.

## Act 3 — Measuring twice (the shock)

11. The real shock shows up when you measure twice.
12. Keep only the spin-up beam, and ask the same z-question again.
13. Every atom answers up — certainty, probability one, collapse is stable.
14. Now take that certain beam and ask about x instead.
15. The answer is a perfect coin flip — fifty-fifty, every time.
16. An atom certain in z is completely uncertain in x.
17. Ask z, then x, then z again.
18. The final z answer is random — the x question erased the z certainty.
19. Measuring one question destroyed the answer to the other.

## Act 4 — What it means (PAYOFF)

20. This is not a clumsy instrument disturbing delicate atoms.
21. Spin along z and spin along x cannot hold definite values at once.
22. That impossibility is the deepest rule in quantum mechanics, stamped on a plate in 1922.
