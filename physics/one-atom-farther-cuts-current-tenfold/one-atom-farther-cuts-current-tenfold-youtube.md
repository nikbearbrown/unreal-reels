Why One Extra Atom of Distance Cuts a Tunneling Current Tenfold — Bear's Doodles

## Description
A scanning microscope can feel a single atom's bump on a surface. The trick is that moving the tip one atom farther away nearly tenths the current.

A sharp tip floats a tiny gap above a bumpy row of atoms. Electrons tunnel across the gap, giving a small current. That current dies exponentially with the gap — each equal step of distance divides it by ten. Now drag the tip across the surface at a fixed height. Over each atom the gap narrows and the current leaps; between them it collapses. A change in height too small to see becomes a tenfold change in the signal. That brutal sensitivity is exactly what lets the microscope see single atoms. One atom closer, ten times the current — that's how we photograph atoms.
