## Script (one sentence = one beat)

**INTRO**
> Bear's Notes

> What Wave–Particle Duality Actually Means

**HOOK — the cartoon, destroyed by its own dimensions**
1. Ask anyone to draw an electron and you'll get the same cartoon: a little ball with ripples around it.
2. It feels perfect — a solid particle core wearing a wavy coat.
3. But measure both parts: a lab electron's ripples span a tenth of a nanometer, while the ball would be tens of thousands of times smaller.
4. The coat is a stadium and the ball is a grain of dust — this cartoon cannot be the thing.

**INSTANCE 1 — two questions, two answers (shown moving)**
5. So interrogate the electron itself, with two different questions.
6. Ask "how do you travel?" — send many through two slits — and the answer comes back striped, the signature of a wave.
7. Ask "where are you?" — put up a screen — and each answer is one whole dot, never half an electron, never a smear.

**INSTANCE 2 — the two comfortable stories, both wrong (shown moving)**
8. Try the story "it's really a particle with a secret route" — then two slits should give two heaps, and they don't.
9. Try the story "it's really a spread-out wave of stuff" — then a detector should sometimes catch a fraction, and it never does.
10. Both comfortable stories fail: the electron is neither a tiny marble nor a material ripple.

**ABSTRACTION — the one honest picture, named as an endpoint**
11. Here is the honest picture: between the gun and the screen there is one object — a wave.
12. That wave is not electron-stuff smeared through space; it is a map of possibility — brighter means more likely.
13. The dot is not the wave shrinking back into a ball; it is the answer you forced by asking where.
14. That is all duality means: wave-shaped possibilities, particle-shaped results — one object, two kinds of question.

**PAYOFF — the cartoon repaired**
15. Now repair the cartoon: erase the little ball, keep the wave — and let the dot exist only at the moment of arrival.
16. The electron isn't sometimes a wave and sometimes a particle; it is always the wave in between, and always a whole dot when asked.

**NUMBER + SCALE SHIFT**
17. The sizes make it vivid: that wave spans zero point one six seven nanometers — atom-spacing scale, exactly why crystals can reveal it.
18. And between your questions the wave is the only thing evolving — every chip and laser is engineered by computing the wave, never by tracking little balls.

**BOUNDARY + exercise**
19. We haven't yet written the wave down, normalized it, or found its equation of motion — that's the next two videos.
20. Try it yourself: find a ball-with-ripples picture in a textbook or video, and say precisely what it gets wrong.
