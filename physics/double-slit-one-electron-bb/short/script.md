> One Electron Interferes With Itself

1. Fire electrons at two slits one at a time — each arrives as a single dot.
2. Ten in, the dots look random; common sense says two heaps, one behind each slit.
3. Seventy thousand in: stripes — bright and dark bands no two-heap picture allows.
4. Stripes are what waves do: ripples through both slits overlap and cancel.
5. Particles can't do that — a ball takes one slit and joins its heap.
6. And these electrons flew alone: never a second one in the machine to interfere with.
7. So each electron's wave goes through both slits and interferes with itself.
8. The dot is where it's found; the stripes map where finding it is likely — psi squared.
9. Peek at which slit it took, and the stripes vanish — information kills them.
10. Molecules of two thousand atoms paint the same stripes; your wavelength is just too small to show it.
