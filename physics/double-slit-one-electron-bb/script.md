## Script (one sentence = one beat)

**INTRO**
> Bear's Notes
>
> One Electron Interferes With Itself

**HOOK — the buildup begins, unsolved (zero vocabulary)**
1. Fire electrons at two slits so slowly that only one is ever in the machine — each arrives as a single dot.
2. Ten electrons in, the dots look like random specks — no pattern at all.
3. Common sense says they should pile into two heaps, one behind each slit.
4. Keep firing, and something impossible begins to show.

**INSTANCE 1 — what waves do (shown moving)**
5. Ripples passing through two openings overlap — crest on crest brightens, crest on trough cancels.
6. So a wave hitting two slits paints stripes on the far screen: strong, weak, strong.

**INSTANCE 2 — what particles should do (shown moving)**
7. A ball can't overlap with anything: it takes one slit or the other.
8. Balls through two slits stack into exactly two heaps — nothing in between, no stripes.

**TRANSFORM — the buildup (the key case, parametrized)**
9. Now watch the real electrons: two hundred dots, and a faint clustering appears.
10. Six thousand dots, and unmistakable stripes are forming.
11. Seventy thousand dots: clean bright and dark bands — a full interference pattern.
12. Lay the wave's stripe pattern over the dots, and it fits perfectly.
13. And there are bands where electrons never land — forbidden zones no two-heap story can explain.
14. Remember: the electrons flew alone — there was never a second electron to interfere with.

**ABSTRACTION — named only now, as endpoints**
15. So each electron's wave passes through both slits at once and interferes with itself.
16. The dot is just where the electron is found; the stripes map where finding it is likely.
17. That likelihood map has a name: psi squared, the square of the electron's wave function.
18. And the stripe spacing is set by the electron's wavelength — lambda equals h over p, de Broglie's rule.

**PAYOFF — hook resolved + the twist**
19. One electron arrives like a particle and travels like a wave — both, every single time.
20. Peek at which slit it took, and the stripes vanish — not because you jostled it, but because you know.

**NUMBER / SCALE SHIFT**
21. This isn't just electrons: molecules of two thousand atoms have painted the same stripes.
22. You have a wavelength too — walking, about ten to the minus thirty-five meters, far too small for any doorway to reveal.

**BOUNDARY + exercise**
23. We didn't say what the wave function actually is, or how crystals first revealed matter waves — each is its own video.
24. Try it yourself: compute your own de Broglie wavelength, h over m times v, on your next walk.
