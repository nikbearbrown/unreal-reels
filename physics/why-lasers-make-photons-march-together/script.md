Why Lasers Make Photons March Together

A00. A laser starts with atoms pumped full of extra energy.
A01. Each excited atom can release that energy as light.
A02. One passing photon can make another atom emit.
A03. The new photon copies the first photon's direction.
A04. It also copies the photon's color, phase, and polarization.
A05. That copying is called stimulated emission.
A06. Mirrors send the light back through the atoms again.
A07. Every pass gives more excited atoms a nudge.
A08. The matching photons multiply into a bright crowd.
A09. Photons are not labeled like tiny coins.
A10. Two photons in the same state count as one shared pattern.
A11. Two different states leave fewer matching paths.
A12. Quantum bookkeeping favors photons joining an occupied state.
A13. An excited atom is therefore easier to trigger by matching light.
A14. The cavity keeps rewarding the same photon pattern.
A15. A small opening lets part of that pattern escape.
A16. The escaping stream is coherent laser light.
