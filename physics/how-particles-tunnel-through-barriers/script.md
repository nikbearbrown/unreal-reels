How Particles Tunnel Through Barriers

A00. A classical ball cannot cheat a hill.
A01. It climbs only as high as its starting energy allows.
A02. A taller barrier turns the far valley into forbidden territory.
A03. Quantum particles are described by waves, not tiny certainty dots.
A04. Most of the wave stays in the first valley.
A05. A thin tail leaks into the barrier.
A06. Sometimes that tail reaches the far side.
A07. When we look, the particle may appear beyond the wall.
A08. The chance is small, but not zero.
A09. A wider barrier makes the leaked tail fade faster.
A10. A lower barrier lets more of the wave survive.
A11. The particle can even be found inside the forbidden region.
A12. That is not a hidden tunnel in the mountain.
A13. It is the wave refusing to stop instantly.
A14. In atoms, this lets electrons spend time inside nuclei.
A15. Tunneling turns impossible paths into tiny probabilities.
