Why a Wave Packet's Wiggles Move at Half the Speed of the Packet — Bear's Doodles

A quantum particle travels as a little blob of waves — a moving lump full of ripples. Watch closely and the ripples inside slide backward, even as the blob races forward.

Here's the packet: a smooth envelope holding a train of wave crests. The envelope is where the particle actually is, and it glides forward at the particle's speed. Tag one crest with a dot and watch it against the moving envelope. The crest moves forward too, but only half as fast as the envelope. So relative to the blob, the tagged crest slides steadily toward the rear. Fresh crests keep appearing at the front and melting away at the back. The crest speed is the phase velocity; the envelope's is twice it, the group velocity. What you see and measure is the blob — the particle rides the envelope, not the ripples.
