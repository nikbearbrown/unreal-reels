## Title
Why Mixing Two Frozen Quantum States Makes Probability Slosh — Bear's Doodles

## Description
Each energy state on its own has a probability cloud that just sits there, perfectly still. Add two of those still clouds together, and the result sloshes side to side.

Here's a particle trapped in a box. Its lowest energy state has one smooth hump of probability that never moves. The next state up has a two-lobed cloud — also frozen in place. Each one is secretly spinning underneath, but at its own steady rate set by its energy. Now add the two states together into one combined cloud. As the two clocks drift apart, the combined cloud sloshes from one side of the box to the other. The sloshing keeps time with the gap between the clocks — the difference in their energies. Two states that each go nowhere, mixed together, make probability move.
