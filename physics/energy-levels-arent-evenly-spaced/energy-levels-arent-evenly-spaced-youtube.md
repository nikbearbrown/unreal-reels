## Title
Why Quantum Energy Levels Aren't Evenly Spaced — Bear's Doodles

## Description
You'd expect a ladder of energy rungs to be evenly spaced, like any normal ladder. But a quantum box's rungs fan apart the higher you climb.

On the left, a wave in a box; on the right, its energy on a ladder. The lowest state fits one half-wave, and its energy is one rung up. Add a second half-wave and the energy doesn't double — it quadruples, to four. Three half-waves land at nine; four land at sixteen. So the rungs sit at one, four, nine, sixteen — and the gaps keep widening. The reason: each step packs in one more half-wave, so the wavelength shrinks. And energy depends on the square of how many waves fit, so doubling them quadruples it. That's why the rungs fan apart instead of marching up in even steps.
