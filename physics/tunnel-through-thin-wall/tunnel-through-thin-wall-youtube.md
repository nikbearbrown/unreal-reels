## Title

## Description
Roll a ball at a hill too steep to climb, and it always rolls back down. Aim a quantum particle at a wall it can't cross — and sometimes it's already on the far side.

A quantum wave travels toward a barrier it hasn't the energy to climb. Classically it must stop and bounce — but a wave can't just cut off at the wall. Inside the barrier the wave stops oscillating and instead decays, fading deeper in. A thick barrier lets it fade to almost nothing before reaching the far side. But make the barrier thin, and the wave still has leftover height at the far edge. From that leftover, a full traveling wave relaunches on the other side. Its smaller height means a small but real chance the particle appears beyond the wall. It never climbed the wall; it leaked through — the barrier was simply thin enough.
