# mini-bio — how long should it be?

**Duration is an output, not an input.** You do not write a bio "to 30 seconds" or "to a
minute." You decide how many beats the story needs, give each beat the time its content
requires, and the runtime is whatever they sum to. A simple figure lands around 30–60s; a
layered life with several ideas, quotes, and turns can run up to ~5 minutes. Both are
correct. Forcing a clock target does one of two harmful things: it *compresses* rich
content below the time the viewer needs to absorb it, or it *pads* simple content with
filler. Neither helps.

```
video_duration = Σ(beat_duration_i)        # the timing contract makes this automatic:
                                           # each beat's screen time = its narration length
```

## Beat-duration taxonomy
Set each beat's length from what it carries, not from a stopwatch. These are floors-and-
ranges, not quotas. (Grounded in cognitive-load / segmentation research: working-memory
consolidation takes ~1–2s per item; novel info decays in ~15–30s without rehearsal; the
more interacting elements a beat carries, the more time it needs.)

| Beat content | Interacting elements | Beat length | Notes |
|--------------|----------------------|-------------|-------|
| Title / who they are | very low | 4–8s | one chunk; narration carries it |
| Soul-ID portrait beat | low | 4–10s | establish or recall the figure; emotional, not dense |
| World / scene-setting | low | 5–10s | mood and place; brief by design |
| A single quote or line | low–moderate | 6–12s | give the words room to land |
| A turning point / "why they mattered" | moderate | 8–18s | a claim plus its consequence |
| An equation / idea built in steps | high | 15–30s **per step** | the transient-information effect is strongest here; do not rush |
| Legacy / "their impact today" | low–moderate | 6–14s | what endures; can breathe at the close |

## Consolidation floors (never cut below these)
Cutting a beat shorter than its floor doesn't make the film faster — it makes that beat
fail to register.

- A single new name / term / date: **~4–5s**
- A quote or a turning point: **~8s**
- A step in a multi-step idea: **~12s**

## Ceiling (never pad above these)
Once the narration for a beat has finished and its idea has landed, move on. Decorative
motion, time-filling graphics, or a held empty card past the line's end is extraneous load.
The beat ends when the thought is complete.

## Putting it together (worked sizing)
- **Short (~30–60s, 5–7 beats):** open soul-id → title → one world beat → one quote/idea →
  one legacy beat → close soul-id. One clean idea, told well.
- **Medium (~1–2.5 min, 8–14 beats):** add a second idea or quote, a `date`, and 1–2
  interior soul-id beats so the figure recurs.
- **Long (~2.5–5 min, 14–24 beats):** three clear movements (who / why / impact), each
  opening on a soul-id beat, each with its own world/quote/date material. Reserve the
  longest beats for any built-up idea or a closing reflection. This is the register of the
  dialogue bios (two voices trading beats).

## A note on Shorts vs. long-form
A 9:16 Short suits short/medium bios. A 4–5 minute bio is a long-form piece — render the
16:9 and don't force it into a Short. Don't split a continuous line of reasoning across two
videos just to hit a length; the cut between would sever the very thread that teaches.
